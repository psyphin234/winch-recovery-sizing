## Source policy (owner's rule, non-negotiable)

- **Engineering, standards, manufacturers' published data and best practice, and published recovery methods only. No broscience:** no forum, club, retailer-blog or "everyone says" figures.
- Every number lives in `js/data.js` as a rule's `params`, next to its sources, the exact words quoted from the source (`quote`) and a `checked` date. `calc.js`, `app.js` and the HTML read them from there; don't hard-code 1.5, 8%, 25% etc.
- Where nothing citable exists, add a `gaps` entry and say "no published figure" on the page instead of guessing. Current gaps: rock (the user picks the nearest published ground, labelled as their choice), snow (not offered), suction, duty cycle.
- Rule `category` drives the tag: method | maker | engineering | horticulture ("Tree care", the RHS) | calculation. **calculation** = this site's own physics; it must cite `physics` and say so where shown.
- Before adding a claim, read the source itself. Checked and dropped on 2026-10-11: "a strap rated over 3x GVM won't stretch enough" (unverifiable), WreckMaster's "body mire 1.5x" (only quoted second-hand on forums, so "bogged to the chassis" uses I4WDTA instead), and an early "within 10% of measured" claim for I4WDTA (true only for the bogged tests; their slight-incline test came out ~30% under, which the slope rule now says).

## Sources used (all in data.js)

- **I4WDTA, Recovery Resistance Calculations (2020)**: total resistance = ground (weight / GCF, after Sibly's NZ 4WD Handbook) + gradient (1/60 per degree to 45°; the page uses weight × sin, which matches) + damage resistance + 25% margin. The page's three bog levels: **not bogged** = GCF; **bogged to the axles** = GCF − 1 (I4WDTA's rule); **bogged to the chassis** = no wheel can roll, damage resistance = full weight added to the ground (how I4WDTA worked out their "bogged to both diffs" field tests, 1–11% above measured). Their worked example (3 500 lb Jeep, soft clay, one wheel, 15° = 4 375 lb) is a test.
- **WARN**: 1.5 × GVWR winch rule; pull by layer (rated on layer 1); min 5 wraps steel / 10 synthetic; ZEON 10 spec and first-layer chart (via 4x4 Down Under's listing); engine running, 650 CCA, lithium caution; heavy-duty snatch block (two WLLs: 4:1 ASME B30.26 and 2:1 BS 7901, MBS 96 000 lb).
- **Crosby G-209**: shackle WLL table, ultimate ≥ 6 × WLL. **LEEA COPSULE 7.5.6**: 8% per sheave.
- **WARN Basic Guide to Winching** (rigging page): tree trunk protector (nylon strap, attachment point, "protects living trees"), never wrap the rope back onto itself, as low as possible, shackle through both ends and the hook loop, back the pin off ½ turn. **RHS, Ring-barking**: loss of bark all or nearly all round the trunk disrupts sap flow, can kill the tree, may not show at first. Both read from the pages themselves on 2026-10-12.
- The drum model (rope centre one rope diameter further out per layer) reproduces WARN's ZEON 10 layer table to within 0.5%; a test checks it. The page draws every winch with the ZEON 10's drum (80 × 229 mm) and 9.5 mm rope, and scales the ZEON 10 chart to the user's rating; it says so under the readouts.

## The page (one page, no modes)

One `.stage` card, top to bottom:
1. **Try:** example chips (`scenarios` in data.js; made-up figures).
2. **Scene** (`V.scene()`): animated side view. The **mass**, **winch** and **anchor distance** fields are HTML overlays on the picture (on the roof, in the sky in front, and on a dimension line over the rope), placed by `toPercent()` so they follow the slope; under 860 px `placeFields()` in app.js moves them below the picture and the dimension line shows the distance as text. The anchor field and the one on the rope diagram are the same value. No force arrows or load/winch labels on the picture (owner's call, 2026-10-11): the balance bars below it make that comparison. Slope turns the world (`rotate`, shrinks up to 30% at 45° so the tree stays in view); bogging sinks the vehicle into the ground (the ground is drawn over it); ground chips change the ground pattern (colours are illustration only). Animations by status: winching (wheels turn, vehicle edges forward, rope runs), close (slower), short (rope strains, winch arrow pulses red), rolls (downhill). All off under prefers-reduced-motion.
3. **Controls:** ground chips: Tar, Gravel, Grass, Sand (I4WDTA loose wet sand, 3: the owner chose the cautious sand figure), Mud (their shallow mud, 3; deeper is "How deep", and mud to the axles gives 2 = their deep mud), Soft clay, Rock (asks for the nearest ground). Hard wet sand, loose wet sand, shingle, deep mud and snow were dropped from the list (owner's call); older saved choices fall back to the default. how deep (3 icon chips), slope (Uphill/Downhill + slider 0–45°, shows ° and %). The rope angle (slider 0–45° with a little top view that swings) sits in the rope section above the top-view diagram (owner's call, 2026-10-12); the rope must pull resistance ÷ cos, and the verdict states the sideways pull, resistance × tan; its extra pull is the magenta `--series-4` segment, validated with the other three). No typed angles (owner's call).
4. **Verdict:** headline, detail, a one-click fix when another line count or a closer anchor would work, and the balance bars (rope pull needed, segmented, with a tick at the estimate without margin; winch on its start layer, with a mark for its end layer).
5. **How the rope is run:** three clickable thumbnails (single/double/triple, each with the winch pull and a pass/fail mark, or "rope too short"), the top-view rigging diagram with the **anchor distance typed on the rope** (with a rope angle, everything from the vehicle's front out is sheared so the ropes leave at the true angle; a dashed "way it must move" line and a magenta arc show the angle; the rope is drawn shorter at steep angles to keep the height down, and the anchor's label moves above the tree; an anchor-side block's label is placed to stay clear of the Anchor label, the distance box, a triple line's end strap and the "way it must move" line: see the comment in `rigging()`), and what each block / tree protector / rope end holds: "this pull" and "Rate it for" (the load if the winch pulls its full rating, which the blocks multiply too; the owner asked why the two differ, 2026-10-12), with a Crosby shackle size for the rate-it-for load.
6. **The pull, metre by metre:** (the current and power charts were dropped on 2026-10-12, owner's call; the pull chart stays, and the readouts under it count up live while it plays: amps now with the peak, Ah and Wh so far, kW now, winching time, metres moved) the winch from the front (motor, drum between flanges, gearbox, tie bar, feet; labelled) with the drum cut through, one circle per wrap (grey = must stay on, blue = on at the start, light = wound on during the pull) and the rope leaving the outermost wrap "to the anchor", plus a legend; it sits beside the gauge, charts below with **rope length on the drum** (no steel/synthetic choice since 2026-10-12: the page always keeps 5 wraps on, the owner's call; the `min-wraps` rule records WARN's 10 for synthetic); current gauge; pull chart (available vs needed) and current chart sharing the distance axis, with a playhead; Play button (plays once when it scrolls into view, 6 s); "the vehicle must move [x] m"; readouts (time, peak A, Ah and Wh drawn, peak kW, rope wound in). **Power** (rule `power`, physics): drawn = 12 V (the ZEON 10 chart's voltage) × current, at the rope = rope pull × rope speed; shown live under the gauge as two bars with the heat lost, and as a third chart. All three charts use end labels so their distance axes line up. No battery or wiring questions (owner's call); amps, Ah and kW are what the winch draws.
7. **Winch for this pull:** first a card for **your winch** as typed (rating, whether it copes with this pull, and how far over or under the minimum rating it is; a prompt if none is typed), then the minimum rating with where it comes from (larger of WARN's 1.5 × mass and this pull on its start layer); the user's winch **layer by layer** (a bar per layer against a line for the pull needed, the layers the pull uses highlighted, and a plain note on why outer layers pull less); common sizes for the chosen line count, each with a box per layer (green enough, red not, outlined = used in this pull). No "copes" label (removed 2026-10-12, owner's call).
8. **Synthetic winch rope: strength and WLL** (added 2026-10-12, owner's request): GP-12 (Southern Ropes, Cape Town; spliced strength from its datasheet, which doesn't say minimum or average; the table's rows were checked against the PDF's word positions) and Samson AmSteel-Blue (minimum spliced strength) at 8–12 mm, as bars with the WLL part solid (strength ÷ 5, Samson's warning statement; Southern gives no WLL, so Samson's factor is applied to both and the page says so), lines for the winch's full pull and this pull, and a table with "× your winch". Steel wire rope isn't shown: no clean maker's table for winch-rope sizes was found (2026-10-12).
9. **Tree protector: what it is and why** (added 2026-10-12, owner's request): a photo of a ring-barked tree, two small top-view drawings (✓ strap + shackle, ✕ rope round the tree hooked back on itself; side by side, stacked under 520 px), then what it is, why (tree, rope), how to rig it (WARN) and what it must hold (this pull and rate-it-for, the same figures as the tree protector card under the rope diagram, which links here). Then the disclaimer and explainers (layers, block and shackle MBS/WLL, gaps).

- **Winch rating as typed** (`C.parseRating`): "EW9500" → 9 500 lb; "Zeon 10", "11XP" → thousands of lb (model-number reading, shown as such); "4.5 t", "4.T" → tonnes; "12 000 lb", "5443 kg", "9.5k", "45 kN"; a bare number ≥ 1000 → lb, ≤ 10 → tonnes; a unit beats a bare number. The page always shows its reading under the field and asks for a unit when unclear.
- Status: pass if the winch covers the need including the 25% margin all the way; amber "Close" if it covers the estimate but not the whole margin; red if not, or if the rope can't reach the anchor and keep its minimum wraps.

## Structure

```
index.html        The page (one stage) + explainers
sources.html      Every rule with category, summary, quote, sources, checked date; gaps; source list
js/data.js        THE data file: sources, rules, grounds (+ how each is drawn), bog levels, Crosby shackles, WARN ZEON 10 data, gaps, scenarios, defaults, common winch sizes
js/calc.js        Pure calculations (no DOM): resistance, parseRating, rigging, drum/layers, chart interpolation, motor, simulate(), sizing, shackleFor
js/ui.js          DOM + formatting helpers (el(), kg/lb/kN, lbMin/kgMin round UP for minimums, "Sources:" line)
js/diagrams.js    scene, lineThumb, rigging, drum, gauge, lineChart (playhead + tooltip). Rigging and charts are drawn at their box's real width so text stays readable on phones
js/app.js         State, building the controls once, update(), playback
js/sources.js     Builds sources.html from data.js
css/style.css     The SA Towing Check stylesheet (base) + this tool's section at the end. Chart colours --series-1/2/3 were validated (colour-blind separation) on --surface-2; the site's --accent was too light for a series colour
img/              ring-barked-sycamore.jpg (CC BY-SA 2.0, credited in the caption)
tests/            index.html + calc.test.js (data, I4WDTA example, bog levels, rating parser, rigging statics, WARN layer table, chart, simulation); run.ps1 runs them in headless Edge
```
